## 3. Procedural Time Limit Rules (Muddatlarni Hisoblash)

### Criminal Procedure Code (1994)

**Articles 314–317** – Procedural deadline calculation  
https://www.lex.uz/en/docs/-111460?ONDATE=21.02.2025

- **Article 314** (Muddatlarni hisoblash): Establishes framework for measuring timeframes in criminal proceedings; specifies how days, months, and years are counted when procedural deadlines are established.
- **Article 315** (Time during coercive measures): Time periods computed when procedural coercive measures (detention, restrictions) are in effect.
- **Article 316** (Extension): Authorized officials may prolong procedural deadlines under specified circumstances.
- **Article 317** (Restoration): Missed procedural periods may be reinstated subject to established conditions.

**Status:** Full text requires direct document fetch; article titles and purposes documented.

### Civil Procedure Code (2018)

**Articles 152–155** – Calculating procedural time limits  
https://lex.uz/docs/5535095

- **Article 152** (Calculation): Time limits measured in days, weeks, months, or years. When a limit begins from an event, calculation starts the **day after** that occurrence. **If a deadline falls on a non-working day, it extends to the next business day.** Time limits in months/years end on the corresponding date of the final month; if no such date exists, the limit ends on the last day of that month.
- **Article 153** (Omission consequences): Procedural actions after expiration may be disregarded; court can restore missed limits under Article 155.
- **Article 155** (Extension and restoration): Courts may extend or restore lapsed deadlines if party demonstrates legitimate reasons for non-compliance.

**Key rule: Non-working day deadline auto-extends to next business day.**

### Economic Procedure Code (2018)

**Articles 119–124** – Procedural deadline calculation  
https://lex.uz/en/docs/-3523891?ONDATE=01.04.2018+00

- **Article 119** (Setting and computing): Courts establish time periods per Code and other legislation; rules for calculation and application throughout proceedings.
- **Article 120** (Deadline expiration): Specifies when procedural time limits expire.
- **Article 121** (Consequences of omission): Outlines consequences when parties miss established time requirements.
- **Article 122** (Suspension): Permits suspension of time limits under specified circumstances.
- **Article 123** (Restoration): Enables restoration of missed procedural deadlines when valid reasons demonstrated.
- **Article 124** (Extension): Authorizes extension of procedural deadlines when circumstances warrant additional time.

### Law on Appeals of Individuals and Legal Entities

**Article 11** – Review timeframes  
https://lex.uz/acts/-75892

"**Petitions and Complaints:** are resolved within **one month** from submission to the state body. For matters not requiring substantial investigation, deadline is **no later than 15 days.** When special investigations are necessary, the responsible body may extend the deadline, but total review period cannot exceed **two months.**"

**Key rule: Standard 30 days; expedited 15 days if no investigation needed; maximum 60 days total.**
